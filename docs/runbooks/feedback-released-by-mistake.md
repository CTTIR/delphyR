# Feedback released by mistake

Released feedback is immutable: it is what participants saw when they rated.
A mistake is corrected by a new version, never by changing the released one.

## Recognise

- A released summary misstates the contributions, shows text that should have
  been redacted, or belongs to another analysis.
- *Rounds and analysis → Correct released feedback* lists the released versions of a round.

## First

1. Establish who has seen it. The audit trail records every display of a
   feedback version to a participant with its time.
2. If the text reveals a person or other content that must not be shown,
   treat it as a [security incident](security-incident.md) as well.
3. If the next round is not yet open, do not open it. If it is open, deciding
   whether to continue is the study lead's call; the software does not stop
   the round.

## Resume

1. An editor creates a corrected editorial version; a second person releases
   it. The author of a version cannot release it.
2. The study lead creates a corrected feedback from the same analysis with the
   released versions, reviews it and publishes it as a correction with a
   rationale, a statement of the effect on ratings already given, and a note
   for participants. All three are required.
3. From then on every view shows the corrected version with the note. Ratings
   already saved stay as they are; earlier displays keep referring to the
   earlier version.
4. Decide whether participants who already rated are asked to review their
   rating, and record that decision in the study documentation.

What cannot be undone: participants who saw the earlier version have seen it.
The report lists both versions and which one replaced the other.

## Responsible

Study lead; an independent reviewer for the release.

## Evidence

Both feedback versions with their hashes, the correction record with its three
texts, the display events of the earlier version and the documented decision.
